## 2. THE 12 UPGRADES (implemented in this repo)

### A. Retention Architecture 2.0
The 2026 algorithm's heaviest signal is **average % watched** (50%+ = strong, 70%+ = exceptional), and the first 30 seconds decide everything.
- **Re-hook every 30–40s:** a question, a visual shift, or a "but" — scripted, not improvised. Every scene block in the film package is tagged with its retention job.
- **Open-loop ledger:** every film opens exactly 1 loop in the hook and closes it in the final 10%. Nothing open = boring; two open = confusing.
- **The 8-second scene rule stays**, but scenes must *alternate shot scale* (wide → macro → medium) to reset viewer attention.
- **Kill the middle:** if a scene doesn't raise stakes or pay a promise, delete it — even if it's pretty.

### B. Velocity Engineering (the first 48h decide the video's fate)
New uploads are tested on a small pool in the first 24–48 hours. Engineering that test:
1. **Premiere the video** (not silent upload) — Premieres concentrate your first-hour viewers into one live room = velocity spike.
2. **Community post 2h before** with a poll ("Lions vs sleeping zebra — who wins? 🦁🦓") — warms the notification pool.
3. **Publish window:** **16:00–18:00 IST** (hits EU evening + US midday — the two highest-value English audiences) on **Tue/Fri/Sun**.
4. **Reply sprint:** answer every comment in the first 60 minutes (comment velocity is a ranking input).
5. **Pinned comment with a question** within 1 minute of going live ("Which rule surprised you most? 👇").
6. **Shorts hand-grenade:** 1 Short from the film goes live **1h after** the long-form, with "full film on the channel" — Shorts are 90% of new uploads on YouTube and pushed to non-subscribers; they're the discovery engine for a zero-follower channel.

### C. The Shorts → Long Funnel (v1's biggest gap)
For a new faceless channel, Shorts are the *subscriber machine*, long-form is the *revenue machine*. The hybrid is the standard 2026 play:
- Every long film → **3 Shorts** cut from its strongest moments (scripted in the film package).
- Shorts structure: hook in the **first 1 second** (not first 3), 25–40s runtime, captions always on (most watch muted), loop-friendly ending (last line flows into first line).
- Every Short: related-video link to the parent film + a comment pinned by you.

### D. Session-Time Architecture
YouTube rewards channels that *continue* sessions:
- **Series, not videos:** every film ends with "Rule #N" (numbered series = playlists = binge).
- **End screen:** exactly 1 best-performing own video + 1 subscribe (never leave it empty).
- **Playlists:** "The Predator's Rules" (main series), "60-Second Wild" (Shorts), per-habitat playlists later.

### E. Test Everything Packaging
- Use YouTube's built-in **thumbnail Test & Compare** (3 thumbnails per video — the 3 variants for Film #1 are already in this repo).
- Title/thumbnail must *complement, not repeat*: thumbnail = emotion, title = curiosity gap. Never the same words in both.
- Weekly packaging review: CTR < 4% after 72h → repackage (new thumbnail or title) before making a new video.

### F. Trend-Surfing System (see Section 3 — this is the trending-page engine)

### G. Global Timing Science
English content, made from India:
- Primary publish slot: **16:00–18:00 IST** = 12:30–14:30 CET / 06:30–08:30 ET — EU evening + US morning commute.
- Secondary: **22:30 IST** = US evening prime.
- Never publish Fri night IST (dead EU weekend slot for long-form).

### H. Multilingual Reach (free views)
Turn on **auto-dubbing** for every upload (Studio → Settings → Upload defaults → allow auto-dub). Your documentary format dubs beautifully — this alone can 2–3× foreign views in months. Add Spanish + Hindi manual tracks once a video proves itself.

### I. Authority Signals (trust = watch time = ranking)
- 3–5 real sources in every description (journals, NatGeo, BBC) — viewers who trust you watch longer and comment more.
- Chapters on every film.
- **Altered content disclosure on every upload.** Paradoxically, transparency *increases* watch time (viewers stop suspecting and start watching).

### J. The Data Loop (weekly decision ritual — Sundays, 20 min)
| Metric reads | Decision rule |
|---|---|
| CTR < 4% | Repackage thumbnail/title (Test & Compare), keep video live |
| Retention cliff in first 30s | Hook problem → rewrite next 3 hooks shorter + louder |
| Retention cliff mid-video | Pacing problem → cut scene length by 20% next film |
| AVD ≥ 50% + CTR ≥ 5% | **Scale trigger:** make 2 more in that exact format within 7 days |
| A format underperforms twice | Kill it, rotate in a new format test |
| A Short outperforms its film | Remake that film's angle as next long-form (validated demand) |

### K. Buffer Batching (never miss a slot)
- Before launch: **3 complete films banked.** Batch days: script 3 films Monday, generate all week, edit Saturday.
- A missed upload costs more than a mediocre upload — the algorithm (and habit) punishes gaps.

### L. Engagement Engineering
- Every film plants **one debate question** ("Would you rather be a zebra or a lioness for a day?") — comments in hour 1 = velocity.
- Heart + reply to first 20 comments daily for 48h.
- Community tab unlocked at launch-week: polls 2×/week (polls are the highest-reach community post type).

## 3. WHAT IT ACTUALLY TAKES TO TREND IN 2026

The trending/browse system rewards **five things** v1 didn't address:

1. **Velocity in the cold-start window (24–48h).** Views-likes-comments per hour, weighted by *non-subscriber* engagement. → Implemented via Premiere + Community + Shorts grenade + reply sprint (Upgrade B).
2. **The CTR × AVD product.** 10% CTR with 20% AVD loses to 6% CTR with 60% AVD. Target: **CTR ≥ 5% × AVD ≥ 50%.** → Implemented via packaging tests + retention architecture.
3. **Session continuation.** Videos that lead to *another watch* get pushed. → Implemented via series numbering, playlists, end screens.
4. **Topical momentum.** Trending pages (and Suggested) favor what's being searched *this week*. → Trend-surf system below.
5. **Format-native Shorts presence.** The Shorts shelf is where unknown channels get discovered; the browse system promotes channels with cross-format momentum. → Shorts funnel.

### The Trend-Surf System (run every Monday, 30 min)
**A. The annual spike calendar** (plan films 2 weeks ahead of each spike):
| Date | Event | Film angle |
|------|-------|-----------|
| Feb 27 | World Polar Bear Day | *Why Polar Bears Hunt Humans (and When They Don't)* |
| Mar 3 | World Wildlife Day | State-of-the-wild epic |
| Apr 25 | World Penguin Day | *The Penguin's 60-Mile Death March* |
| Jun 16 | World Sea Turtle Day | *The Beach That Eats 1-in-1000 Turtles* |
| Jul 29 | International Tiger Day | *The Tiger That Walked 3,000 km* |
| Aug 10 | World Lion Day | Lion content (your forte) |
| Aug 12 | World Elephant Day | *Why Elephants Never Forget a Face* |
| Mid-Jul–Aug | **Shark Week** | 2–3 shark films scheduled in advance |
| Nov–Jan | Serengeti migration season (Great Migration coverage peaks) | *The River Crossing That Kills 6,000 Wildebeest a Year* |

**B. The news-jack protocol (24h turnaround):**
1. Monday scan: Google News → "lion attack / shark / elephant rescue / wildlife viral" + Google Trends rising queries.
2. If an animal story is spiking and fits your style → run Prompt #3 with the event as the spine → ship a film or 3 Shorts within 48h while search volume is hot.
3. Title format that rides spikes: `[Event] Explained in [N] Minutes` or `What Really Happened to [Animal]`.
4. Rule: only news-jack what you can tell *better* than news outlets — through the animal's decision-making. You're not a news channel; you're the *why* channel.

**C. The evergreen anchor:** 70% of output stays evergreen (search-able questions that rank for years); 20% trend-surfs; 10% experiments. Trending moments bring spikes; evergreen brings the floor that makes a channel valuable.

## 4. THE 90-DAY EXECUTION PLAN

### Phase 0 — Bank the ammunition (Days 1–7)
- [ ] Channel live: handle, logo, banner, description, auto-dub on.
- [ ] Film #1 (package in `film01_launch_package.md`) produced, edited, premiered.
- [ ] Films #2 and #3 fully banked (backlog titles below).
- [ ] 6 Shorts banked.
- [ ] First community post + channel trailer = Film #1.

### Phase 1 — Calibration (Weeks 2–3)
- Cadence: **2 films + 4 Shorts/week** (Tue/Sun films, Mon/Thu/Sat Shorts).
- Publish across 3 different formats from the 4 (Predator Decision / Great Escape / Hidden Science / Last Stand).
- Sunday ritual: apply decision rules (Section 2-J). No emotional decisions midweek.

### Phase 2 — Double down (Weeks 4–6)
- The Sunday data picks your winner format. 2 films/week, **same format**, until CTR or AVD plateaus.
- Start series numbering ("The Predator's Rules #3"), playlists, end screens wired to the best video.
- First trend-surf film (calendar or news-jack).
- Target checkpoints: 1 video > 10K views; channel CTR ≥ 5%; 500+ subs.

### Phase 3 — Scale what works (Weeks 7–10)
- If 2+ videos have AVD ≥ 50%: raise to 3 films/week (batching makes this possible in the same hours).
- Add 1 collab: 3 similar-size AI-wildlife channels — shared shoutouts/end-screen swaps.
- Spanish + Hindi manual dubs on your top 3 videos.
- Target checkpoints: 1 video > 100K views; 1,000 subs; YPP application submitted.

### Phase 4 — Compound (Weeks 11–13)
- YPP ads live → revenue reinvested: better Veo tier for finals, thumbnail designer tool.
- Launch second series (habitats / "Last Stand" spinoff).
- Quarterly review: kill bottom 20% of effort, scale top 20%.

### The Views Ladder (what "catchy" realistically looks like)
| Stage | Milestone | Typical timing with v2 discipline |
|-------|-----------|-----------------------------------|
| 1 | First video > 1K views | Weeks 1–3 |
| 2 | First video > 10K views | Weeks 3–6 |
| 3 | First video > 100K (the "breakout ticket") | Weeks 5–10 |
| 4 | 1K subs + 4K hours → monetized | Month 2–4 |
| 5 | Repeatable 100K+ system | Month 4–6 |

Every stage is a probability game — the ladder is climbed by *shipping + repackaging*, not by one perfect video. Most channels that die, die at stage 1–2 because they quit before the algorithm finished testing them (videos can surface months later).
